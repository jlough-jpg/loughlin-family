READ BEFORE YOU FOLLOW A PIN

A historic address is a research lead.

The supplied guide notes Philadelphia’s street renumbering under an 1856 ordinance, effective in 1857. Modern house-number searches remain provisional until checked against dated directories or historic maps. Family associations below are attributed to the guide unless supporting evidence is identified.

135 and 127 Shippen Street are excluded from navigation. Their modern locations remain unresolved; do not substitute the same numbers on Bainbridge Street.

DOCUMENTED ENTRIES · UNRESOLVED LOCATIONS

The Shippen Street directory clues.

The guide reports two directory matches for a Dennis Sweeney. An entry documents that name at an address; it does not, by itself, identify him as Ellen’s father.

135 Shippen · 1843

The guide transcribes: “Sweeney Dennis, gent., 135 Shippen.” “Gent.” means gentleman; the entry does not include “E.”

1843 directory · digitized text ↗

Search the text for “Sweeney Dennis.” No navigation pin assigned.

127 Shippen · 1846

The guide transcribes: “Sweeney Dennis, gent., 127 Shippen.” The modern parcel remains unresolved.

1846 directory · digitized text ↗

Search the text for “Sweeney Dennis.” No navigation pin assigned.

Family notes also report a fire in Dennis’s stables in 1850. The event has not been independently confirmed, and the notes do not identify which address held the stables.
